# IEEE S&P 2027 Text Rebuttal — Working Draft

We thank the reviewers for recognizing LAMP's core idea and identifying where our claims and interface were imprecise. We agree that related-work comparison and input-encoding validity are the two highest-priority issues.

**Positioning and comparison.** LAMP is not a general-purpose IOP or end-to-end zkML system, but a specialized checker for certified matrix-product commitments. It combines Freivalds with sampled folds over interleaved codewords; CP-SNARK and CP-Link bind samples to pre-challenge commitments. Ligero, Brakedown, Blaze, and Orion provide code-testing foundations; zkMatrix and zkMaP are the closest statement-level comparisons; zkCNN, zkGPT, and zkLLM prove broader neural-network computations.

We chose Freivalds-in-Groth16 to compare the same relation, curve, backend, hardware, and security parameters, thereby isolating our reduction. This controlled baseline is insufficient as a state-of-the-art comparison. We will expand Sections 2 and 7 with a table comparing statement, prover work, circuit cost, verifier work, proof size, setup, assumptions, and input model. LAMP, zkMatrix, and zkMaP retain quadratic native prover work, but LAMP reduces the in-circuit relation to `O(tk + E_code)` constraints. Its verifier/proof trade-off is less favorable: LAMP uses `O(t log n)` Merkle work and `O(t)` QALink pairing terms, while zkMatrix and zkMaP report logarithmic and constant verifier time, respectively. We will explain statement/security-model mismatches and not present cross-platform timings as directly comparable. We will add a normalized measurement only if parameters can be matched; otherwise, we will provide the complexity table and explain why empirical comparison would mislead.

**Validity of the committed matrix encodings.** The reviewers are correct: the current online relation applies `EncCheck` only to the intermediate vectors, and validity of the row-wise encodings of `A,B,C` is not discharged by LAMP itself. The sampled folds do not replace this requirement. Our intended setting was a certified-input interface, but the manuscript did not define this boundary clearly enough. We will define a certification relation `R_cert` whose accepted proof establishes the existence of matrices and openings such that `rtABC` commits to their valid row-wise encodings. For proof-based certification, the revised theorem will compose the errors as `epsilon_total <= epsilon_cert + epsilon_LAMP`. An alternative trusted-registration instantiation instead makes encoder/registrar honesty an explicit assumption; we will not conflate that trust assumption with cryptographic certification. Without either interface, arbitrary roots are outside the claimed language. We will state this boundary throughout the abstract, construction, and security analysis.

If raw matrices must also be used inside the same SNARK, one direct bridge is to check each of the `3k` encoded rows against its raw row. With our barycentric Reed--Solomon check this adds `O(k(k+n)) = O(k^2)` constraints at fixed rate, eliminating the linear-circuit advantage. A reusable certification proof may instead bind raw matrices to encoded roots once, but its cost is amortized only when those roots are genuinely reused; we will state this condition rather than assume it. This bridge is relevant to end-to-end zkML when weights or activations also feed nonlinear layers. We will therefore describe LAMP as an online checker that is most beneficial when certified encoded commitments already exist, report certification separately from the measured online cost, and clarify that authenticating `rtABC` as the intended model weights is an application-level provenance obligation.

**Security scope and setup.** The theorem proves the public-coin interactive protocol. We will specify the two-stage transcript order and domain separation, and limit the non-interactive claim to what is justified in the random-oracle model. We will also document Groth16's per-circuit setup and QALink's configuration-dependent CRS, reporting their costs or listing them as deployment limitations.

**Measurements and presentation.** The correct values are `0.06 s`, `8.43x`, and `1.77x`; we will fix all inconsistent occurrences. “Linear” refers to the main constraint count, not total prover time: intermediate computation and encoded-column commitments remain quadratic at fixed code rate. We will report the GPT-2 result as a trade-off—`1.77x` faster proving, but `6.52 s` versus `0.49 s` verification and `3.34 MB` versus `196 B` proofs—and explain that Merkle openings and CP-Link cause the larger proof. We will also clarify encoding-time accounting, add repetitions/variance and memory where available, and strengthen the proof of Theorem 6.2 and its appendix pointer.
