# IEEE S&P 2027 Text Rebuttal — Working Draft

We thank the reviewers for their constructive feedback.

**Positioning and comparison.** LAMP checks matrix-multiplication statements over committed row-wise encodings; it is not a general-purpose IOP or end-to-end zkML system. Ligero, Brakedown, Blaze, and Orion provide general code-testing foundations; zkCNN, zkGPT, and zkLLM target broader neural-network computations. We will compare their statements and input models and clarify that our contribution is the matrix-specific reduction.

Freivalds-in-Groth16 isolates our reduction under the same relation, curve, backend, hardware, and security parameters, but is not a state-of-the-art baseline. zkMatrix reports `O(k^2)` prover work and `O(log k)` transcript/verifier. DualMatrix reports `O(K+k)` field operations plus `O(k)` group operations/pairings, where `K=nnz(A)+nnz(B)+nnz(C)=Theta(k^2)` for dense inputs, and `O(log k)` transcript/verifier. zkMaP reports `O(k^2)` prover work, 320-byte proofs, a constant pairing count, and `O(k)` verifier-side field work. At fixed code rate, LAMP uses `O(k^2)` field work for intermediate vectors and `O(k^2)` group work to commit encoded columns, but reduces the in-circuit relation to `O(tk + E_code)` constraints. With no public zkMatrix implementation and an unavailable zkMaP artifact, rebuttal-period reimplementation would not support reliable like-for-like evaluation. We therefore compare them asymptotically and benchmark the public zkMatrix follow-up DualMatrix as the closest reproducible reference. At `k=1024`, DualMatrix/LAMP proving times were `19.06/7.11 s`, verification times `0.15/0.06 s`, and proofs `88,844/43,332 B`. For the currently implemented proving and verification paths, LAMP was lower on all three metrics at every tested size from `k=128` to `k=8,192`. These LAMP measurements predate the additional `B` test and will be updated; we do not present them as final end-to-end results of the revised protocol. We will tabulate statements, operation types, costs, proof size, setup, assumptions, input models, and experimental conditions.

**Validity of the committed matrix encodings.** Reviewers C and D are correct that the current theorem assumes validity of the committed row-wise encodings rather than proving it. Intuitively, a Brakedown-style test folds row-wise encodings fixed by a commitment with random coefficients: words far from the interleaved code remain far except with small probability and are exposed by random queries, while words within the decoding radius determine unique decoded rows. For `A` and `C`, we will prove a separate Reed--Solomon proximity lemma for the structured challenge `v(r)`. The `B` fold is different: its coefficients are `x=v(r)A`, so a rank-deficient `A` can hide malformed `B` components in its kernel. We will add an independent Brakedown-style test for `B`. After `rtABC` is fixed, the verifier samples a fresh independent random vector `gamma_B`. Before `I` and the code-check points are sampled, the prover computes `b_gamma=gamma_B B` and commits to its encoding together with those of `x,y,z`. `EncCheck` certifies that `hat(b_gamma)=Enc(b_gamma)`, while the sampled equations link it to the row-wise encoding `hat(B)` fixed by `rtABC`: `hat(b_gamma)[j]=<gamma_B,hat(B)[*,j]>`. The revised theorem will give the full proximity/extraction bound. This adds one `O(k^2)` native vector--matrix product, one encoding/commitment, `O(tk+E_code)` circuit constraints, and `O(k)` verifier work; total prover work remains `O(k^2)`, while the circuit remains linear in `k` for fixed `t` and code rate.

If the original matrices are also used as witnesses in the same SNARK circuit, the matrices decoded from the committed row-wise encodings must additionally be linked to those original-matrix witnesses. One straightforward bridge checks all `3k` rows and costs `O(k(k+n)) = O(k^2)` constraints at fixed rate, eliminating the linear-circuit advantage. This arises when model weights or activations also participate in nonlinear relations. We will report this cost separately; establishing that `rtABC` is the commitment to the verifier-intended model weights remains a separate application-level question.
