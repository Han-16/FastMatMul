# IEEE S&P 2027 Text Rebuttal — Working Draft

We thank the reviewers for their constructive feedback.

**Positioning and comparison.** LAMP is a specialized checker for encoded matrix-product commitments, not a general-purpose IOP or end-to-end zkML system. Ligero, Brakedown, Blaze, and Orion provide more general code-testing foundations; zkCNN, zkGPT, and zkLLM target broader neural-network computations. We will compare statements and input models rather than equate end-to-end timings, and clarify that our contribution is the matrix-specific reduction, not code testing itself.

We chose Freivalds-in-Groth16 under the same relation, curve, backend, hardware, and security parameters; it isolates our reduction but is not a state-of-the-art baseline. zkMatrix reports `O(k^2)` prover work and `O(log k)` transcript/verifier. DualMatrix reports `O(K+k)` field operations plus `O(k)` group operations/pairings, where `K=nnz(A)+nnz(B)+nnz(C)=Theta(k^2)` for dense inputs, and `O(log k)` transcript/verifier. zkMaP reports `O(k^2)` prover work, 320-byte proofs, and a constant pairing count, although its detailed analysis includes `O(k)` verifier-side field work. At fixed code rate, LAMP has `O(k^2)` intermediate-vector field work and `O(k^2)` encoded-commitment group work, but reduces the in-circuit relation to `O(tk + E_code)` constraints. We could not locate a public zkMatrix implementation, and zkMaP's artifact was unavailable. Reimplementing either system within the rebuttal period would not permit a reliable like-for-like evaluation. We therefore compare both systems asymptotically and benchmark DualMatrix, a publicly implemented follow-up to zkMatrix, as the closest reproducible experimental reference. At `k=1024`, DualMatrix/LAMP proving times were `19.06/7.11 s`, verification times `0.15/0.06 s`, and proofs `88,844/43,332 B`. For the currently implemented proving and verification paths, LAMP was lower on all three metrics at every tested size from `k=128` to `k=8,192`. These LAMP measurements predate the additional `B` test and will be updated; we do not present them as final end-to-end results of the revised protocol. We will add a table comparing statements, operation types, costs, proof size, setup, assumptions, input models, and experimental conditions.

**Validity of the committed matrix encodings.** Reviewers C and D are correct that the current theorem assumes input-codeword validity rather than proving it. Intuitively, Brakedown tests many encoded rows at once by taking a random linear combination after the rows have been committed. If the committed rows are not close to a valid row-wise encoding, then, except with small probability, the combined word is also far from the code, and random coordinate queries expose the discrepancy; if they are close, the proof extracts the corresponding decoded rows. For `A` and `C`, we will prove a separate Reed--Solomon proximity lemma for the structured challenge `v(r)`. The `B` fold is different: its coefficients are `x=v(r)A`, so a rank-deficient `A` can hide malformed `B` components in its kernel. We will add an independent Brakedown-style test for `B`. After `rtABC` is fixed, the verifier samples a fresh independent random vector `gamma_B`; the prover commits to `b_gamma=gamma_B B` and its encoding before the query positions are sampled. `EncCheck` certifies the folded encoding, while the sampled equations check `hat(b_gamma)[j]=<gamma_B,hat(B)[*,j]>`. The revised theorem will give the proximity/extraction bound; revised costs will include the extra encoded fold, `EncCheck`, `O(tk)` sampled constraints, and `O(k)` verifier challenge processing.

If raw matrices are also used inside the same SNARK, the decoded matrices need an additional bridge to those witnesses. One straightforward bridge checks all `3k` rows and costs `O(k(k+n)) = O(k^2)` constraints at fixed rate, eliminating the linear-circuit advantage. This is needed when weights or activations also participate in nonlinear relations. We will report this cost separately; authenticating `rtABC` as the designated weights remains application-level provenance.
