\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented $\protocol$, a matrix-multiplication checking layer whose SNARK
relation has size $\mathcal{O}(tk+t\log n+E_{\mathsf{cc}}(k))$ and becomes
$\mathcal{O}(tk)$ with a linear-time code-commitment backend.
The construction combines Freivalds' randomized reduction with local proximity
checks over linear error-correcting codes, and its security theorem explicitly
accounts for SNARK soundness, backend codeword binding, commitment binding,
Freivalds failure, and proximity-sampling error.

Our implementation confirms the expected checking-layer separation against a
Freivalds SNARK baseline with the QA-linked intermediate-vector backend:
at $k=4{,}096$,
$\fmQA$ gives a $30.29\times$ constraint reduction and a $7.18\times$
proving-time speedup; a GPT-2 workload case study gives a $2.30\times$
constraint reduction and a $1.77\times$ proving-time speedup.
The evaluation is intentionally scoped to the online checker after certified
input commitments are available, matching the committed-input interface used in
the theorem.
Within this model, the implementation covers the intermediate-vector
$\codeComV$ relation through pre-query commitments, in-circuit encoding checks,
Merkle membership, and QA-NIZK/CP-linking.
Stricter query counts such as $t=130$ or $t=133$ follow directly from the
security parameter formula, while engineering optimizations can further reduce
verification overhead and batch many matrix multiplications.
