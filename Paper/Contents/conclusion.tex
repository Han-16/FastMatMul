\section{Conclusion and Related Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Our work complements verifiable ML systems such as
SafetyNets~\cite{ghodsi2017safetynets}, Mystique~\cite{weng2021mystique},
zkCNN~\cite{liu2021zkcnn}, vCNN~\cite{lee2024vcnn}, and
zkML~\cite{chen2024zkml}; sumcheck/GKR-based VC~\cite{goldwasser2015delegating};
dedicated matrix-verification protocols~\cite{cong2024zkmatrix,
cong2026dualmatrix,zhang2025zkvc,bennett2023matrix}; and code-based
SNARK/proximity systems~\cite{ames2017ligero,golovnev2023brakedown,
xie2022orion,ben2018fast}.
Unlike full-circuit ML systems or polynomial-commitment matrix
arguments~\cite{kate2010constant}, $\protocol$ is a dedicated multiplication
checking layer over certified encoded roots and is orthogonal to succinct
backends, including universal/updatable-SRS and inner-product-style
systems~\cite{maller2019sonic,chiesa2020marlin,bunz2018bulletproofs}.
The security theorem accounts for SNARK soundness, message/leaf binding,
CP-link consistency, Freivalds failure, and proximity-sampling error.

The artifact confirms the expected separation. At $k=4{,}096$, $\fmQA$ gives a
$30.29\times$ constraint reduction and a $7.24\times$ proving-time speedup over
Freivalds-in-SNARK; on the GPT-2 case study it gives a $2.30\times$ constraint
reduction and a $1.78\times$ proving-time speedup over certified roots.
The remaining limitation is explicit: the artifact measures the online checking
layer but assumes certified encoded input roots for raw matrices. Closing that
pipeline, reporting key/CRS sizes, reducing verification overhead, and batching
many multiplications are the main deployment steps.
