\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented $\protocol$, a matrix-multiplication checking layer whose SNARK
relation has size $\mathcal{O}(tk+t\log n+E_{\mathsf{cc}}(k))$ and becomes
$\mathcal{O}(tk)$ with a linear-time code-commitment backend.
The construction combines Freivalds' randomized reduction with local proximity
checks over linear error-correcting codes, and its security theorem explicitly
accounts for SNARK soundness, backend codeword binding, commitment binding,
Freivalds failure, and proximity-sampling error.

Our implementation confirms the expected checking-layer separation against a
Freivalds SNARK baseline: at $k=4{,}096$, $\fmQA$ gives a $30.29\times$
constraint reduction and a $7.18\times$ proving-time speedup; a GPT-2 workload
case study gives a $2.30\times$ constraint reduction and a $1.77\times$
proving-time speedup.
The main limitation remains explicit: the current artifact measures the
fold/opening/linking layer under a conditional code-commitment backend model and
does not yet independently implement or measure standalone $\codeComV$.
Closing that backend gap, reducing verification overhead, and batching many
matrix multiplications are the primary next steps toward a full deployment.
