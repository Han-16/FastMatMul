\section{Implementation and Evaluation}
\label{sec:implementation}

We implement the multiplication-checking layer of $\protocol$ and evaluate the
current artifact, denoted $\fmQA$, against an optimized SNARK-in-circuit
Freivalds baseline~\cite{freivalds1977probabilistic}.
$\fmQA$ uses multi-column Merkle openings, a Groth16 circuit proof, and a
QA-NIZK linking layer for the opened encoded columns.
All main experiments use a 256-bit prime field, rate $\rho=1/2$, codeword
length $n=2k$, and $t=128$ sampled positions.
The experiments were run on a Linux server with an AMD EPYC 7B13 CPU
(32 cores) and 254\,GB RAM.

These measurements are layer-level measurements under the conditional
linear-time code-commitment backend model of Section~\ref{sec:construction}.
They cover the implemented fold, opening, and linking terms of the
multiplication-checking layer, but they do not independently implement or
measure a standalone $\codeComV$ component.
Consequently, the empirical claims below should not be read as complete
end-to-end deployment costs from raw inputs.
Appendix~\ref{app:eval-details} reports the full benchmark table, measurement
coverage, parameter details, and dense-code encoding costs.

\paragraph{Primary matrix benchmark}
Figure~\ref{fig:main-benchmark} shows the main scaling behavior.
Freivalds constraints grow by approximately $4\times$ with each doubling of
$k$, matching the expected $\mathcal{O}(k^2)$ scaling.
In contrast, $\fmQA$ grows by approximately $2\times$, matching the
$\mathcal{O}(k)$ checking-layer scaling under the conditional linear-time
backend model.
The crossover in absolute constraint count occurs at $k=256$.
At $k=1{,}024$, $\fmQA$ uses $419{,}139$ constraints versus $3{,}156{,}298$
for Freivalds, a $7.53\times$ reduction.
At $k=4{,}096$, $\fmQA$ uses $1{,}667{,}305$ constraints versus
$50{,}495{,}818$ for Freivalds, a $30.29\times$ reduction.

The same benchmark shows a proving-time crossover at $k=256$.
At $k=4{,}096$, $\fmQA$ proves in $56.44\,\text{s}$ versus
$405.04\,\text{s}$ for Freivalds, a $7.18\times$ speedup.
$\fmQA$ also completes $k=8{,}192$ with $3{,}331{,}779$ constraints and a
$178.48\,\text{s}$ proving time; Freivalds was not measured at that size in the
current artifact.

\paragraph{Verification, proof size, and GPT-2 workload}
$\fmQA$ verification is nearly constant in the matrix benchmark at about
$0.10\,\text{s}$, dominated by QA-NIZK link verification.
Freivalds verification is smaller in absolute terms, between $1$ and $2$\,ms,
because it verifies a direct Groth16 proof without sampled-column linking
material.
The current artifact therefore trades slower verification for much smaller
prover circuits and faster proving at medium and large dimensions.
As Figure~\ref{fig:proof-size} shows, the $\fmQA$ proof grows from
$15{,}108$ bytes at $k=128$ to $104{,}388$ bytes at $k=4{,}096$ and
$125{,}508$ bytes at $k=8{,}192$.

Figure~\ref{fig:gpt2-case-study} summarizes a GPT-2-style workload with
sequence length $1{,}024$, $36$
matrix-multiplication claims, and $5$ commitment groups, $\fmQA$ reduces
constraints from $76.8$ million to $33.5$ million ($2.30\times$) and proving
time from $408.69\,\text{s}$ to $230.33\,\text{s}$ ($1.77\times$).
Setup time is also lower ($2{,}144.47\,\text{s}$ versus $4{,}173.32\,\text{s}$).
Verification remains slower for $\fmQA$ ($4.94\,\text{s}$ versus
$0.49\,\text{s}$), so latency-sensitive deployments still need batching,
amortization, or a more succinct opening/linking backend.

\paragraph{Summary}
The experiments support the claimed asymptotic separation for the
multiplication-checking layer and show concrete gains over the Freivalds SNARK
baseline at large $k$.
They do not close the remaining end-to-end backend gap: the standalone
code-commitment backend relation $\codeComV$ is still modeled rather than
independently implemented and measured.
