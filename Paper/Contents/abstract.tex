Verifiable computation systems frequently need to prove large matrix multiplication statements, yet this primitive remains a severe bottleneck for SNARK-based approaches: na\"ive arithmetization of a $k \times k$ product incurs $\mathcal{O}(k^3)$ constraints, and even the classical Freivalds reduction yields $\mathcal{O}(k^2)$.

We present $\protocol$, a matrix-multiplication checking layer for verifiable computation that reduces the matrix-checking SNARK circuit size to $\mathcal{O}(tk + t\log n + E_{\mathsf{cc}}(k))$, where $t$ is a security-parameter-dependent query count and $E_{\mathsf{cc}}(k)$ is the verification cost of a selected code-commitment backend.
If that backend has a linear-time verifier, the checked relation becomes $\mathcal{O}(tk)$.
The protocol combines a structured variant of Freivalds' randomized check with \emph{proximity testing over linear error-correcting codes}: matrices are encoded row-wise and committed via Merkle trees; the verifier detects any inconsistency by querying $t$ random codeword positions and checking lightweight algebraic fold relations.
The expensive $\mathcal{O}(k^3)$ matrix arithmetic is performed entirely outside the SNARK.
We prove that this checking layer achieves perfect completeness and computational soundness, relative to certified encoded input commitments and the selected code-commitment backend, with error $\epsilon_{\mathsf{SNARK}} + \epsilon_{\mathsf{cc}} + \epsilon_{\mathsf{bind}} + (k-1)/|\mathbb{F}| + 4(1-\delta)^t$, where $\delta$ is the relative distance of the underlying code.
The Fiat--Shamir non-interactive variant incurs the standard additional random-oracle loss $\epsilon_{\mathsf{FS}}(q_H)$.
This gives a complete online checking protocol for the standard committed-input setting: the verifier starts from certified encoded commitments to the input matrices, and the checker proves the multiplication relation among those committed objects.

We implement the multiplication-checking layer of $\protocol$ in Rust with a QA-linked code-commitment instantiation: the prover commits to the intermediate vectors before the query set is derived, checks Reed--Solomon encoding consistency in the circuit, proves Merkle membership for sampled leaves, and CP-links the circuit values to the externally committed leaves.
We evaluate this implementation against a direct Freivalds SNARK baseline.
In our QA-NIZK implementation, experiments show a $7.5\times$ constraint reduction at $k = 1{,}024$ and a $30.3\times$ reduction at $k = 4{,}096$ for this layer.
At $k = 4{,}096$, $\fmQA$ achieves a $7.18\times$ proving-time speedup and produces a $104$\,KB proof; it continues to $k = 8{,}192$ with a $126$\,KB proof.
A GPT-2 workload case study with $36$ matrix-multiplication claims shows a $2.30\times$ constraint reduction and a $1.77\times$ proving-time speedup.
