Verifiable computation systems frequently need to prove large matrix
multiplication statements, but na\"ive SNARK arithmetization of a
$k \times k$ product costs $\mathcal{O}(k^3)$ constraints and even Freivalds'
classical reduction leaves $\mathcal{O}(k^2)$ circuit work.
We present $\protocol$, a matrix-multiplication checking layer that reduces the
SNARK relation to $\mathcal{O}(tk+E_{\mathsf{msg}}(k))$ constraints plus
$\mathcal{O}(t\log n+E_{\mathsf{link}}(k))$ auxiliary opening/linking work,
where $t$ is a security-parameter-sized constant.
The protocol combines Freivalds' randomized check with proximity testing over
row-wise encoded matrix commitments; in our CP-linked instantiation, message
commitments fix intermediate vectors before sampling and public wire handles
link Groth16 witness values to compact Merkle leaf handles.

We prove perfect completeness and computational soundness relative to certified
encoded input commitments and the intermediate binding/linking backend, with
error
$\epsilon_{\mathsf{SNARK}} + \epsilon_{\mathsf{cc}} + \epsilon_{\mathsf{bind}}
+ (k-1)/|\mathbb{F}| + 4(1-\delta)^t$.
The Fiat--Shamir variant adds the standard random-oracle loss
$\epsilon_{\mathsf{FS}}(q_H)$.
The checker is modular: it verifies multiplication over certified roots, while
raw-input certification is supplied by the surrounding system.

We implement the checking layer in Rust and compare it with a direct Freivalds
SNARK baseline.
At $k = 4{,}096$, $\fmQA$ gives a $30.3\times$ constraint reduction, a
$7.18\times$ proving-time speedup, and a $104$\,KB online proof excluding
setup/key/CRS material; it continues to $k = 8{,}192$ with a $126$\,KB online
proof under the same convention.
A GPT-2 checking-layer case study with $36$ claims over certified roots shows a
$2.30\times$ constraint reduction and a $1.77\times$ proving-time speedup.
