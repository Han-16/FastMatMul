\section{Implementation and Evaluation}
\label{sec:implementation}

We implement \(\protocol\) in Go using gnark.  The CP-SNARK layer is instantiated as a Groth16 over the BN254 curve, following the commit-and-prove interface used by \cite{legosnark, gnark}.  The encoding layer uses a Reed--Solomon code with rate \(\rho=1/2\), so the codeword length is \(n=2k\), and all matrix experiments use \(t=128\) sampled codeword positions.  The CP-Link layer is implemented using a pairing-based QA-NIZK proof~\cite{qa-nizk}; this instantiation verifies using a constant number of pairings.  Appendix~\ref{app:cplink-instant} gives the CP-Link construction used in our implementation.
The QA-NIZK CP-Link instantiation requires a trusted setup; this is consistent with our backend, since the implementation already relies on a trusted setup for Groth16.
The experiments were run on a Linux server with an AMD EPYC 7B13 CPU
(32 cores) and \(254\) GB of memory.

\subsection{LAMP Component Breakdown}
\label{sec:eval-lamp-breakdown}

Table~\ref{tab:lamp-breakdown} breaks down the measured \(\protocol\) costs by
component.  The column ``ABC commit'' is the time to commit to the encoded
matrix blocks \(\vect{m}_{ABC,j}\), including the Pedersen leaf commitments and
the Merkle root \(\rt_{ABC}\).  The column ``XYZ commit'' is the analogous time
for the encoded intermediate-vector blocks \(\vect{m}_{XYZ,j}\) and the root
\(\rt_{XYZ}\).  Once the Merkle trees are built, the sampled Merkle proof is
generated quickly: in all measured rows, Merkle proof generation is about
\(1\) ms.  The Groth16 proving column is the time to prove the online
arithmetic relation from Figure~\ref{relation:online}, and the CP-Link column is
the time to link the SNARK-side sampled-block commitments to the opened
Pedersen leaves.

For proof size, the Merkle column reports the serialized Merkle multi-proof for
the sampled openings.  The Groth16 column reports the Groth16 proof together
with the SNARK-side witness commitments \(\widetilde{\cm}\).  The CP-Link
column reports the serialized CP-Link proof.

The breakdown shows the expected division of labor in \(\protocol\).  The
Groth16 proving time grows roughly linearly with \(k\), reflecting the
\(\mathcal{O}(tk)\) online relation.  Commitment generation is also visible,
especially for \(\rt_{ABC}\), because each sampled codeword position contains
three length-\(k\) encoded matrix columns before they are packed into Pedersen
leaves.  Verification remains small: the verifier checks one Groth16 proof,
sampled Merkle memberships, and CP-Link proofs, with total verification time
below \(10\) ms in all single-matrix rows.

\subsection{Comparison with a Freivalds Baseline}
\label{sec:eval-freivalds}

We compare \(\protocol\) with a Freivalds baseline implemented as a SNARK
circuit.  The baseline applies the same randomized reduction but computes the
resulting vector-matrix products inside the circuit, so its constraint count
scales as \(\mathcal{O}(k^2)\).  Table~\ref{tab:lamp-vs-freivalds} compares
constraint counts, proving time, verification time, and online proof size.  For
the Freivalds baseline, the proof-size column is \(196\) B, accounting for the
Groth16 proof and the commitment material used by the baseline.

The crossover occurs at \(k=256\).  At \(k=1{,}024\), \(\protocol\) uses
\(418{,}149\) constraints compared with \(3{,}156{,}298\) constraints for
Freivalds, a \(7.55\times\) reduction, and proving time drops from
\(25.63\) s to \(7.19\) s.  At \(k=4{,}096\), the constraint count is reduced
from \(50{,}495{,}818\) to \(1{,}666{,}315\), a \(30.30\times\) reduction, and
proving time drops from \(405.04\) s to \(48.54\) s.  For \(k=8{,}192\),
\(\protocol\) completes with \(3{,}330{,}789\) constraints, \(151.61\) s
proving time, \(7\) ms verification time, and \(67{,}268\) B of proof material.
The Freivalds baseline ran out of memory at this size, so its entries are not
reported.  The price is larger online proof material and slightly higher
verification time, because \(\protocol\) sends sampled Merkle openings and
CP-Link material in addition to the Groth16 proof.


\subsection{GPT-2 Workload}
\label{sec:eval-gpt2}

We also evaluate a GPT-2-style workload with sequence length \(2^{10}=1{,}024\).
The workload contains \(36\) matrix-multiplication claims and \(5\) commitment
groups.  Table~\ref{tab:lamp-gpt2} reports the aggregate constraint count,
proving time, verification time, and online proof size for \(\protocol\) and
the Freivalds baseline.

On this workload, \(\protocol\) reduces the constraint count by \(2.30\times\)
and the proving time by \(1.78\times\).  Verification is \(1.39\times\) slower
than the Freivalds baseline because the verifier also checks Merkle membership
and CP-Link proofs.  The \(\protocol\) proof is larger because it aggregates
the sampled openings and linking material for all \(36\) multiplication claims.
