\section{Implementation and Evaluation}
\label{sec:implementation}

We implement \(\protocol\) and evaluate its performance.  The implementation
follows the construction in Section~\ref{sec:construction} and includes the
independent \(B\)-proximity test.  We instantiate the CP-SNARK with Groth16,
using the commit-and-prove interface~\cite{gnark,legosnark}.  For CP-Link, we
use a pairing-based QA-NIZK proof~\cite{qa-nizk}; the concrete instantiation is
given in Appendix~\ref{app:cplink-instant}.  The implementation is available at
\url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/LAMP-38E3/.}

Our evaluation addresses four questions.  First, does the constraint count of
the revised protocol scale linearly with the matrix dimension?  Second, how
does its concrete performance compare with a matched Freivalds-in-Groth16
baseline and with DualMatrix?  Third, which components dominate its cost?
Fourth, how does it scale when several independent matrix-product claims are
proved together?

\noindent\textbf{Experimental setup.}
We implement \(\protocol\) and the Freivalds baseline in Go using gnark and
BN254.  All experiments were run on a Linux server with an AMD EPYC 7B13 CPU,
32 logical cores, and 254 GB of memory.  We use a rate-\(1/2\) Reed--Solomon
code and \(t=128\) sampled positions.  Matrix-product generation and setup are
reported separately and are excluded from total proving time.  The latter
includes encoding, commitment generation, Merkle opening, Groth16 proving, and
CP-Link proving.

\subsection{Comparison with a Freivalds Baseline}
\label{sec:eval-freivalds}
Table~\ref{tab:lamp-vs-freivalds} compares the revised \(\protocol\) with a
Freivalds-based SNARK circuit.  At \(k=2^7\), the fixed cost of the online
relation makes \(\protocol\) more expensive.  Starting from \(k=2^8\), the
linear constraint growth of \(\protocol\) becomes visible: as \(k\) doubles,
the Freivalds circuit grows by roughly \(4\times\), whereas the revised
\(\protocol\) circuit grows by roughly \(2\times\).  This matches the expected
gap between the \(\mathcal{O}(k^2)\) vector--matrix computation in the baseline
and the \(\mathcal{O}(tk+E_{\mathsf{code}})\) relation in \(\protocol\).

At \(k=4{,}096\), \(\protocol\) reduces the constraint count from
\(50{,}495{,}818\) to \(2{,}238{,}148\), a \(22.56\times\) reduction, and
reduces proving time from \(405.04\) s to \(50.77\) s.  At \(k=8{,}192\), the
Freivalds baseline runs out of memory, while \(\protocol\) completes with
\(4{,}473{,}867\) constraints and a proving time of \(157.41\) s.
Verification remains about \(0.06\) s for \(\protocol\), compared with
\(1\)--\(2\) ms for the Freivalds baseline.  This is the cost of verifying the
Merkle openings and CP-Link proofs in addition to the Groth16 proof.

\subsection{Comparison with DualMatrix}
The zkMatrix and zkMaP artifacts were unavailable, so we use the public
DualMatrix implementation as the closest reproducible dedicated baseline.
Table~\ref{tab:lamp-vs-dualmatrix} reports the common proving- and
verification-time metrics.  At \(k=1{,}024\), \(\protocol\) proves in
\(7.59\) s and verifies in \(0.06\) s, compared with \(18.54\) s and
\(0.15\) s for DualMatrix.  Across all tested dimensions, DualMatrix takes
\(2.40\)--\(4.49\times\) as long to prove and \(2.17\)--\(3.00\times\) as
long to verify.

\subsection{LAMP Component Breakdown}
Table~\ref{tab:lamp-breakdown} separates encoding, commitments, Groth16,
Merkle openings, CP-Link, setup, and peak memory.  The \(XYZB\) column includes
the intermediate vectors used by the independent \(B\)-proximity test.  For
small dimensions, Groth16 proving dominates.  At \(k=4{,}096\), encoding and
commitment generation take \(35.57\) s in total, Groth16 proving takes
\(14.87\) s, and CP-Link proving takes \(0.33\) s.  Peak memory at this point
is \(10.91\) GiB and rises to \(35.93\) GiB at \(k=8{,}192\).  On the verifier
side, Groth16 and Merkle verification remain a few milliseconds, while CP-Link
takes about \(0.05\)--\(0.06\) s.

\subsection{Batching Matrix-Multiplication Claims}
Table~\ref{tab:lamp-batch} reports the revised batched experiment.  Each
additional claim adds exactly \(67{,}664\) constraints, while total proving
time grows from \(0.81\) s for one claim to \(5.47\) s for ten claims.  The
proof remains about \(22\)--\(23\) KB because all claims share the sampled
positions and multi-opening structure.  Verification remains about \(0.06\) s
across the measured batch sizes.
