\section{Implementation and Evaluation}
\label{sec:implementation}

We implement the multiplication-checking layer of $\protocol$ and evaluate the
current artifact, denoted $\fmQA$, against an optimized SNARK-in-circuit
Freivalds baseline~\cite{freivalds1977probabilistic}.
$\fmQA$ uses multi-column Merkle openings, a Groth16 circuit proof, and the
sampled-opening backend from Section~\ref{sec:construction}.
All main experiments use a 256-bit prime field, rate $\rho=1/2$, codeword
length $n=2k$, and $t=128$ sampled positions.
The experiments were run on a Linux server with an AMD EPYC 7B13 CPU
(32 cores) and 254GiB RAM.

These measurements include the online sampled-handle path described in
Section~\ref{sec:construction}: pre-query vector commitments fix
$\vect{x},\vect{y},\vect{z}$ before $I$, vector-level CP-Link connects those
commitments to the Groth16 vector witnesses, the CC-SNARK backend binds every
sampled matrix column or vector leaf to a public SNARK-side commitment, Merkle
openings authenticate compact external leaf handles, and sampled linking checks
connect the sampled views.
The raw sampled matrix columns are SNARK witness data, not serialized auxiliary
proof bytes.
All byte counts in this section are online proof bytes: they exclude raw-matrix
encoding and input-root construction for $\cm_A,\cm_B,\cm_C$,
proving/verifying keys, serialized CRS data, and generator material.
They should not be read as complete end-to-end deployment costs from raw
matrices.
Appendix~\ref{app:eval-details} records parameter and measurement coverage
details.

\paragraph{Primary matrix benchmark}
Figure~\ref{fig:main-benchmark} shows the main scaling behavior.
Freivalds constraints grow by approximately $4\times$ with each doubling of
$k$, matching the expected $\mathcal{O}(k^2)$ scaling.
In contrast, $\fmQA$ grows by approximately $2\times$, matching the
$\mathcal{O}(k)$ checking-layer scaling of the sampled-opening backend.
The crossover in absolute constraint count occurs at $k=256$.
At $k=1{,}024$, $\fmQA$ uses $418{,}149$ constraints versus $3{,}156{,}298$
for Freivalds, a $7.55\times$ reduction.
At $k=4{,}096$, $\fmQA$ uses $1{,}666{,}315$ constraints versus
$50{,}495{,}818$ for Freivalds, a $30.30\times$ reduction.

The same benchmark shows a proving-time crossover at $k=256$.
At $k=4{,}096$, $\fmQA$ proves in $48.54\,\text{s}$ versus
$405.04\,\text{s}$ for Freivalds, an $8.34\times$ speedup.
$\fmQA$ also completes $k=8{,}192$ with $3{,}330{,}789$ constraints and a
$151.61\,\text{s}$ proving time; Freivalds was not measured at that size in the
current artifact. Table~\ref{tab:meow-breakdown} breaks down the $\fmQA$ rows
into commitment, SNARK, Merkle, and CP-Link components.

\paragraph{Verification, online bytes, and GPT-2 workload}
$\fmQA$ verification is nearly constant at about $0.01\,\text{s}$ in the matrix
benchmark, dominated by the auxiliary linking backend; Freivalds verification is
faster because it checks only a direct Groth16 proof.
The artifact therefore trades slower verification for smaller prover circuits
and faster proving at medium and large dimensions.
The $\fmQA$ online proof grows from $22{,}404$ bytes at $k=128$ to
$59{,}332$ bytes at $k=4{,}096$ and $67{,}268$ bytes at $k=8{,}192$.
At $k=4{,}096,t=128$, publishing the three sampled input-matrix columns alone
would require about $3tk\cdot32\approx50$\,MB; Appendix~\ref{app:eval-details}
gives detailed byte, setup, and constraint accounting.

Figure~\ref{fig:gpt2-case-study} and Table~\ref{tab:gpt2-case-study} report the
GPT-2-style workload over input roots. $\fmQA$ reduces constraints by
$2.30\times$ and proving time by $1.78\times$, with lower setup time in this
experiment. Verification is $1.39\times$ slower, so latency-sensitive
deployments still need batching, amortization, or a more succinct
opening/linking backend.
The $3.36$\,MB GPT-2 online proof is larger than the single-matrix rows because
it aggregates $36$ multiplication claims and their sampled SNARK-side
commitments, Merkle authentication paths, and linking material.

\paragraph{Strict parameters and deployment scope}
The measured rows use $t=128$ to study scaling. If $\delta=1/2$, a conservative
per-term $2^{-131}$ proximity budget uses $t=133$, increasing sample-dependent
work by only $1.0391\times$.
At $k=4{,}096$, this extrapolates to at most $1.74$ million constraints,
$50.44\,\text{s}$ proving time, and $62$\,KB online proof bytes.
The current artifact does not include measured $t=133$ rows; these strict
numbers are extrapolations from the measured $t=128$ rows.
Online-byte figures exclude raw-matrix encoding, input-root construction,
proving/verifying keys, and serialized CRS or generator material. End-to-end
deployments preserve the measured prover-time win only if amortized root
generation costs stay below
the observed margins: $356.50\,\text{s}$ for $k=4{,}096$ and $178.55\,\text{s}$
for the GPT-2 row.
The CSV artifact reports point estimates and aggregate setup/proving/verification
times; repeated-run variance, confidence intervals, peak memory, and serialized
key/CRS/generator sizes remain unmeasured.
