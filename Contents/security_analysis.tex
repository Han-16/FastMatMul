\section{Security Analysis}
\label{sec:security}

We prove perfect completeness and computational soundness for the public-coin
interactive \(\protocol\) scheme.  The proof is stated for the protocol in which the
verifier samples the Freivalds challenge, the query positions, and the
code-check points.

\paragraph{Statement and scope}
The CP-SNARK relation is the local relation defined in
Section~\ref{sec:snark-relation}; this section proves why satisfying that
sampled relation, together with the commitment-opening and CP-Link checks,
implies soundness for the matrix-multiplication statement.

The theorem is relative to a certified input root \(\rt_{ABC}\).  Namely,
\(\rt_{ABC}\) binds, before the Freivalds challenge is sampled, to
row-wise encodings
\[
  \widehat{\mat{A}}=\enc(\mat{A}),\qquad
  \widehat{\mat{B}}=\enc(\mat{B}),\qquad
  \widehat{\mat{C}}=\enc(\mat{C})
\]
of fixed matrices \(\mat{A},\mat{B},\mat{C}\in\mathbb{F}^{k\times k}\).
The multiplication statement is false when these certified matrices satisfy
\(\mat{A}\mat{B}\neq\mat{C}\).

After the verifier samples the Freivalds challenge, the prover commits to the
intermediate-vector messages \(\vect{x},\vect{y},\vect{z}\) and to their
encoded views before the verifier samples the query tuple \(I\) and the
code-check points.  This ordering is the only timing property needed by the
proof: all values tested by the sampled proximity checks are fixed before the
sampled positions are known.

\paragraph{Error terms}
We separate the errors into two groups.

\emph{Relation-level statistical errors.}
These are the errors that remain even when the CP-SNARK relation is enforced
correctly on the sampled witness.
The probabilistic Reed--Solomon encoding checks for
\(\widehat{\vect{x}},\widehat{\vect{y}},\widehat{\vect{z}}\)
fail with probability at most \(\epsilon_{\mathsf{code}}\).  For the
Reed--Solomon consistency check used by \(\protocol\), if each code-check
point is sampled uniformly from outside the evaluation domain after the
intermediate commitments are fixed, then
\[
  \epsilon_{\mathsf{code}}
  \le
  \frac{3(n-1)}{|\mathbb{F}|-n}.
\]
The remaining relation-level errors are the Freivalds error
\((k-1)/|\mathbb{F}|\) and the proximity-sampling error
\(4(1-\delta)^t\).

\emph{Protocol-level cryptographic errors.}
These errors ensure that the sampled relation is evaluated on values that
actually come from the committed data.
\(\epsilon_{\mathsf{cp}}\) bounds the soundness error of the CP-SNARK proof
system for the relation in Section~\ref{sec:snark-relation}.
\(\epsilon_{\mathsf{open}}\) bounds failures of the opening layer, including
Merkle position binding and Pedersen binding for opened leaves.
\(\epsilon_{\mathsf{link}}\) bounds CP-Link failures, namely accepting link
proofs in which the CP-SNARK-side committed sampled blocks and the opened
Pedersen leaves do not contain the same messages.

\begin{theorem}[Completeness and soundness]\label{thm:security}
Assume that the input root \(\rt_{ABC}\) is certified as above, that the
CP-SNARK relation is the relation of Section~\ref{sec:snark-relation}, and
that the component errors are bounded by
\(\epsilon_{\mathsf{cp}}\), \(\epsilon_{\mathsf{open}}\),
\(\epsilon_{\mathsf{link}}\), and \(\epsilon_{\mathsf{code}}\).
Then the interactive \(\protocol\) scheme satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item \textbf{Perfect completeness.}
  If \(\mat{A}\mat{B}=\mat{C}\), the honest prover is accepted with
  probability one.

  \item \textbf{Interactive computational soundness.}
  For every \(\ppt\) prover \(\mathcal{P}^*\), if the certified matrices
  satisfy \(\mat{A}\mat{B}\neq\mat{C}\), then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr[\mathsf{Acc}]
&\le
\epsilon_{\mathsf{cp}}(\lambda)
+\epsilon_{\mathsf{open}}(\lambda)
+\epsilon_{\mathsf{link}}(\lambda)
+\epsilon_{\mathsf{code}}(\lambda)\\
&\quad
+\frac{k-1}{|\mathbb{F}|}
+4(1-\delta)^t .
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(t=|I|\) is the number of sampled codeword positions and \(\delta\) is
the relative distance of the code \(\mathcal{C}\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Completeness.}
If \(\mat{A}\mat{B}=\mat{C}\), then for every challenge vector
\(\vect{r}=(1,r,\ldots,r^{k-1})\), the honest intermediate vectors
\[
  \vect{x}=\vect{r}\mat{A},\qquad
  \vect{y}=\vect{x}\mat{B},\qquad
  \vect{z}=\vect{r}\mat{C}
\]
satisfy \(\vect{y}=\vect{z}\).  By linearity of \(\mathcal{C}\), for every
codeword position \(j\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \widehat{\vect{x}}[j]
  &= \fold(\vect{r},\widehat{\mat{A}}[*,j]),\\
  \widehat{\vect{y}}[j]
  &= \fold(\vect{x},\widehat{\mat{B}}[*,j]),\\
  \widehat{\vect{z}}[j]
  &= \fold(\vect{r},\widehat{\mat{C}}[*,j]).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus every sampled fold check and equality check is satisfied.  The honest
prover also supplies valid encoding checks, Merkle openings, CP-Link proofs,
and a valid CP-SNARK proof, so the verifier accepts.

\emph{Soundness.}
Condition first on the complement of the protocol-level cryptographic failure
events and the Reed--Solomon encoding-check failure event.  This conditioning
costs
\[
  \epsilon_{\mathsf{cp}}
  +\epsilon_{\mathsf{open}}
  +\epsilon_{\mathsf{link}}
  +\epsilon_{\mathsf{code}} .
\]
Under this conditioning, the accepted proof has the following properties:
the accepted CP-SNARK proof has a witness satisfying the local relation in
Section~\ref{sec:snark-relation}; the certified root \(\rt_{ABC}\) fixes
row-wise encoded matrices
\(\widehat{\mat{A}},\widehat{\mat{B}},\widehat{\mat{C}}\) before \(r\); the
intermediate-vector commitments fix \(\vect{x},\vect{y},\vect{z}\) and their
encoded views before \(I\); and every sampled value used inside the CP-SNARK
relation is the value opened from the corresponding pre-query commitment.
Moreover, the encoding checks certify the committed encoded views as
Reed--Solomon encodings of their corresponding messages, except with
probability already charged to \(\epsilon_{\mathsf{code}}\).

Assume that \(\mat{A}\mat{B}\neq\mat{C}\), and let
\(\mat{D}=\mat{A}\mat{B}-\mat{C}\neq 0\).
There is a nonzero column \(\vect{d}\) of \(\mat{D}\).
The event \(\vect{r}\mat{D}=0\) implies
\[
  \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} d_i r^i = 0 ,
\]
where the left-hand side is a nonzero polynomial in \(r\) of degree at most
\(k-1\).  Since \(r\) is sampled after \(\rt_{ABC}\) is fixed,
Schwartz--Zippel gives
\[
  \Pr[\vect{r}\mat{D}=0]
  \le
  \frac{k-1}{|\mathbb{F}|}.
\]

Now condition further on \(\vect{r}\mat{D}\neq 0\).  Then no vectors
\(\vect{x},\vect{y},\vect{z}\) can satisfy all four global relations
\[
  \vect{x}=\vect{r}\mat{A},\qquad
  \vect{y}=\vect{x}\mat{B},\qquad
  \vect{z}=\vect{r}\mat{C},\qquad
  \vect{y}=\vect{z},
\]
because these equalities would imply
\(\vect{r}\mat{A}\mat{B}=\vect{r}\mat{C}\).
Therefore at least one of the four encoded relations checked by the sampled
protocol is false.

Consider any one false relation, for example
\(\vect{x}\neq\vect{r}\mat{A}\).  Since the encoding checks have not failed,
the committed vector symbols are coordinates of
\(\enc(\vect{x})\).  By code linearity, the corresponding folded matrix symbols
are coordinates of \(\enc(\vect{r}\mat{A})\).  These two codewords are distinct,
so by relative distance they disagree on at least \(\delta n\) positions.  A
uniform query misses this disagreement set with probability at most
\(1-\delta\), and \(t\) independent queries miss it with probability at most
\((1-\delta)^t\).  The same argument applies to the other three relations.
Union-bounding over the four possible violated relations gives the proximity
term \(4(1-\delta)^t\).

Combining the protocol-level failures, the encoding-check failure, the
Freivalds failure probability, and the proximity-sampling failure probability
proves the stated bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Fiat--Shamir]
The theorem above analyzes the public-coin interactive protocol.  A
non-interactive variant can derive \(r\), \(I\), and the code-check points from
domain-separated transcript hashes via the Fiat--Shamir transform.  Proving
that variant adds the standard random-oracle-model loss and is orthogonal to
the interactive soundness statement above.
\end{remark}
