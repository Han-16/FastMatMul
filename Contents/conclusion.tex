\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We proposed \(\protocol\), a new protocol for verifying matrix multiplication.
The key idea is to move the expensive vector--matrix products outside the
SNARK circuit and replace them with sampling-based proximity tests over linear
codewords.  The prover first encodes and commits to the matrices, then computes
the Freivalds intermediate vectors using a verifier-sampled random challenge.
The prover then encodes and commits to these intermediate vectors, and the
SNARK circuit proves only local fold relations at randomly sampled positions.
Merkle openings and CP-Link proofs ensure that the values used inside the
CP-SNARK are consistent with the committed data fixed before the sampling
challenges are known.

Our implementation confirms the intended tradeoff.  For a fixed number \(t\) of
queries, the main arithmetic relation has \(\mathcal{O}(tk)\) constraints
rather than the \(\mathcal{O}(k^2)\) constraints of a Freivalds baseline.
Thus, when \(t\) is fixed, the constraint count of \(\protocol\) grows linearly
with the matrix dimension \(k\).  In the matrix benchmark at \(k=4{,}096\),
\(\protocol\) reduces the constraint count by \(30.3\times\) and proving time
by \(8.34\times\), while keeping verification below \(10\) ms.  We also show
that, when proving multiple matrix products with the same structure, batching
keeps the Merkle membership proof size almost unchanged.  This demonstrates
that \(\protocol\) can amortize opening overhead effectively when multiple
matrix-multiplication claims share the same leaf layout.

\(\protocol\) can also be applied to verifiable AI applications.  The gains are
smaller for a GPT-2 layer, where the computation consists of rectangular matrix
multiplications of different sizes and smaller matrix products inside each
attention head.  In this setting, it is harder to pack multiple products into a
single Pedersen leaf.  Nevertheless, \(\protocol\) achieves a \(2.30\times\)
constraint reduction and a \(1.78\times\) proving-time improvement over the
Freivalds baseline on the GPT-2 workload.

The main remaining cost of \(\protocol\) is the proof size and the
commitment/opening layer.  In particular, workloads with consecutive matrix
products of different sizes require more commitments, causing sampled opening
proofs and linking proofs to accumulate.  As future work, we plan to optimize
the opening layer to reduce proof size, develop batching and sharing techniques
for consecutive matrix products of different sizes, and exploit the fact that
weights are mostly fixed in verifiable AI scenarios for batching optimizations.
These directions may reduce proof size and verification overhead on more
complex AI workloads while preserving the linear constraints provided by
\(\protocol\).
